## 2.1 Evolution of Fashion E-commerce and Recommendation Systems

Over the years, fashion e-commerce has evolved significantly from simple product catalogs to intelligent, personalized shopping platforms. Early fashion retail systems focused primarily on basic product browsing and filtering by categories or price. However, the explosion of online shopping and big data analytics has transformed the landscape. Modern fashion e-commerce platforms now leverage machine learning and artificial intelligence to provide personalized product recommendations, dramatically improving user engagement and sales conversion rates. Research shows that personalized recommendations increase user satisfaction by up to 40% and drive significant revenue growth in fashion retail [2]. With the growth of mobile technology and improved internet bandwidth, these systems have become more accessible and capable of handling complex computational tasks in real-time. Cloud-based architectures have enabled fashion retailers to scale their recommendation engines efficiently without maintaining expensive infrastructure. These advancements have established the foundation for developing intelligent fashion recommendation systems like DressMate, which aim to provide personalized styling advice at scale.

## 2.2 Computer Vision in Fashion Analysis

Computer vision has become a crucial technology in the fashion industry, enabling systems to automatically analyze clothing items, body characteristics, and styling preferences from images. Early fashion tech systems relied on manual tagging and human expertise, which was time-consuming and subjective. Today, computer vision algorithms can detect body shapes, facial features, skin tones, and clothing attributes from photographs with high accuracy. OpenCV, an open-source computer vision library, provides efficient algorithms for image processing, feature detection, and object recognition that are widely used in fashion applications [3]. Advances in cascade classifiers and contour detection have made it possible to identify body silhouettes and physical characteristics from a single photograph. Additionally, color analysis and extraction techniques using LAB color space have enabled systems to understand human skin tones and recommend harmonious color palettes [4]. Research in computer vision for fashion demonstrates that automated body shape detection and skin tone analysis can significantly improve the accuracy and personalization of clothing recommendations, which is central to the DressMate system's approach.

## 2.3 Deep Learning and Feature Extraction

Deep neural networks have revolutionized image understanding and feature extraction in fashion technology. Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), particularly pre-trained models like ResNet50, can extract meaningful visual features from fashion items with remarkable accuracy [5]. ResNet50, trained on ImageNet, learns hierarchical visual representations that capture textures, colors, patterns, and object structures relevant to clothing. The architecture's residual connections enable deep networks to learn complex features while maintaining training stability [6]. Feature extraction using ResNet50 generates high-dimensional embedding vectors (typically 2048 dimensions) that serve as a compact, numerical representation of visual content. These embeddings enable efficient similarity calculations between different fashion items, allowing systems to find visually similar products from large catalogs in real-time. Studies show that using pre-trained deep learning models for feature extraction reduces computational requirements while maintaining or improving recommendation quality compared to hand-crafted features [7]. In DressMate, ResNet50 embeddings form the backbone of the recommendation engine, enabling fast and accurate matching between user images and catalog items.

## 2.4 Recommendation Algorithms and Similarity Matching

Recommendation systems in fashion typically employ collaborative filtering, content-based filtering, or hybrid approaches. Content-based recommendation systems analyze the features of items and user preferences to suggest similar products [8]. Similarity metrics such as cosine similarity measure the distance between feature vectors, enabling the identification of visually and stylistically similar fashion items. Clustering algorithms like K-Means group similar products into clusters, allowing the system to explore related items efficiently [9]. Research demonstrates that combining deep learning features with clustering and similarity metrics produces superior recommendations compared to traditional approaches [10]. Hybrid approaches that integrate user behavior data, item features, and contextual information (such as body shape and skin tone) further enhance recommendation quality [11]. DressMate implements a content-based approach using ResNet50 embeddings combined with K-Means clustering and cosine similarity calculations, allowing it to provide personalized recommendations based on image features and user characteristics.

## 2.5 Personalization Based on Body Shape and Skin Tone

Personalized fashion recommendations based on body morphology and skin undertones represent an emerging trend in AI-driven fashion systems. Research in fashion psychology and styling science shows that clothing recommendations tailored to individual body shapes and skin tones significantly improve user satisfaction and confidence [12]. Body shape detection using computer vision enables systems to understand how different styles complement various body types, a principle that professional stylists have long applied. Skin tone analysis, using color science and the LAB color space, allows systems to recommend colors that harmonize with individual complexions, improving visual appeal and user satisfaction [13]. Studies in color theory and human perception demonstrate that certain colors enhance appearance based on skin undertone (fair, medium, or deep), which can be automatically detected and leveraged for recommendations [14]. Integrating these personalization factors into a unified recommendation system requires combining computer vision techniques for feature detection with machine learning algorithms for preference learning. DressMate incorporates both body shape and skin tone analysis to provide highly personalized styling recommendations, representing a significant advancement over generic fashion recommendation systems.

## 2.6 Large Language Models and AI Stylist Integration

Recent advances in large language models (LLMs) and generative AI have enabled conversational AI assistants that can provide fashion advice, styling tips, and personalized recommendations through natural language interaction [15]. Models like Google Gemini have demonstrated strong capabilities in understanding fashion context, generating coherent style advice, and explaining recommendations to users in natural language [16]. Integrating LLMs with fashion data and recommendation engines creates an AI stylist that can provide conversational, context-aware guidance. Studies show that users find conversational AI assistants more engaging and helpful than static product recommendations, leading to improved user retention and satisfaction [17]. The ability to ask follow-up questions and receive personalized advice creates a more human-like shopping experience. However, challenges remain in ensuring that AI-generated advice is accurate, consistent, and grounded in the user's specific preferences and body type. DressMate incorporates a Gemini-powered AI stylist that integrates with its recommendation engine to provide conversational, context-aware fashion advice.

## 2.7 Modern API-First Backend Architecture

Contemporary fashion recommendation systems employ API-first architectures that separate the presentation layer from business logic and data storage [18]. FastAPI, a modern Python web framework, enables rapid development of high-performance REST APIs with automatic documentation, type validation, and asynchronous request handling [19]. MongoDB, a NoSQL database, provides flexible schema design suitable for fashion applications that store diverse product attributes, user preferences, and recommendation history [20]. This architecture enables the integration of machine learning models into production systems efficiently. Using a microservices or modular backend approach allows each component (image processing, recommendation engine, user management, AI chat) to scale independently based on demand. Studies on modern web architectures demonstrate that API-first design reduces development time, improves maintainability, and enables seamless integration of machine learning capabilities [21]. DressMate follows this modern architecture pattern, separating the frontend from the backend API and leveraging FastAPI and MongoDB for efficient, scalable operation.

## 2.8 NoSQL Databases and Flexible Schema Design

Traditional relational databases require predefined schemas and complex joins to retrieve related data, which can be inefficient for diverse fashion product attributes and rapidly evolving user preference data. NoSQL databases like MongoDB offer flexible, document-oriented schema design that accommodates varying product attributes without schema migrations [22]. MongoDB stores data as JSON-like documents, enabling natural representation of nested structures such as product specifications, user preferences, and recommendation history [23]. Research demonstrates that document-oriented databases significantly reduce development complexity for applications with semi-structured or rapidly changing data requirements [24]. For fashion applications storing diverse product types (dresses, shirts, accessories) with varying attributes (color, size, material, pattern), MongoDB's flexible schema is more practical than rigid relational structures. Additionally, MongoDB's built-in support for indexing, aggregation pipelines, and horizontal scaling through sharding makes it suitable for large-scale fashion recommendation systems [25]. DressMate leverages MongoDB to store products with flexible attributes (product_type, colour, price, embeddings, cluster), user profiles with dynamic preferences (skin_tone, preferred_colors, wardrobe), and recommendation history with contextual metadata, enabling efficient data operations without schema constraints.
